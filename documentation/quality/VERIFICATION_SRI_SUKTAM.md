## 2. Metrical Structure, Recension Differences & Verse Reconciliation

- **Canonical Core (Verses 1–15)**:
  - Verses 1–12, 14–15 are in **Anushtubh meter** (32 syllables).
  - Verse 13 (`आर्द्रां पुष्करिणीं पुष्टिं...`) is in **Trishtubh meter** (44 syllables: 11-11-11-11).
  - These 15 verses constitute the ancient canonical Khila Suktam acknowledged across both Northern and Southern recensions.
- **Liturgical Concluding Verse (Verse 16)**:
  - Verse 16: `यः शुचिः प्रयतो भूत्वा जुहुयादाज्यमन्वहम् । सूक्तं पञ्चदशर्चं च श्रीकामः सततं जपेत् ॥`
  - Explicitly states: *"He who, being pure and restrained, offers ghee daily and continuously recites the fifteen-versed hymn (सूक्तं पञ्चदशर्चं), will attain enduring prosperity."*
  - This verse proves that the ancient canonical core consists of **fifteen verses (पञ्चदश-ऋचम्)**.
- **Customary Chanting Variations (Verses 17–29)**:
  - In customary temple ritual worship (e.g. Tirumala, Sringeri, Kanchi), an extended series of 13 supplementary verses (including the Mahalakshmi Gayatri, prayers to Manasollasa, and Phalastuti) are recited following verse 16.
- **Resolution**:
  - The digital library records the **16-unit canonical edition** (15 core Vedic Riks with swara accents + verse 16 phalasruti/viniyoga).
  - Supplementary verses 17–29 are categorized under an optional extended liturgical profile.
- **Vedic Accents**:
  - Every one of the 15 canonical verses contains Rigvedic musical accents (Anudatta `U+0952`, Svarita `U+0951`).
