## 2. Metrical Structure & Verse Reconciliation

- **Metrical Pattern**: Classical Epic Anushtubh (अनुष्टुप् छन्दः — 32 syllables per shloka: 8-8-8-8).
- **Structural Analysis of the 31 Verses**:
  1. **Verses 1–3 (Narrative Context)**: Rama stands exhausted on the battlefield; Ravana prepares for battle; Sage Agastya approaches Rama to impart the eternal secret.
  2. **Verses 4–15 (Cosmological Divinity of the Sun)**: Enumeration of Aditya as the embodiment of all gods (Brahma, Vishnu, Shiva, Skanda, Prajapati, Indra, Kubera, Yama, Soma, Varuna, Pitris, Ashvins, Maruts, Manu, Vayu).
  3. **Verses 16–24 (Stotra Proper / Namaskara Litany)**: Solemn prostrations to the Sun under cosmic attributes (`नमः पूर्वाय गिरये पश्चिमायाद्रये नमः... जयति जयभद्राय...`).
  4. **Verses 25–28 (Viniyoga & Phalasruti)**: Instructions to recite this hymn in times of peril, wilderness, sickness, and battle. Guarantees victory over Ravana.
  5. **Verses 29–31 (Purification & Re-Entry into Battle)**: Rama sips water thrice (Achamana), gazes upon the sun, takes up his bow, and returns with immense vigor; the Sun god blesses Rama with divine joy ('Jaya!').
- **Verse Count Reconciliation**:
  - Exactly **31 verses** in both the Nirnaya Sagar 1902 edition and the Baroda Critical Edition.
  - All 31 verses are integral to the liturgical narrative.
