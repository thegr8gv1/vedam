## 1. Selection Methodology & Criteria

In strict accordance with the Phase 10 Charter, five records have been selected from the 752-record legacy gap ledger using four objective criteria:
1. **Tier 1 Vedic Core Prioritization:** Priority given to canonical Vedic hymns forming the liturgical foundation of the repository.
2. **Commentarial Traceability:** Selection restricted to compositions where definitive historical bhashyas exist (Sayanacharya, Bhatta Bhaskara Mishra, Mahidhara, Sri Vidyaranya Swami).
3. **Deficiency Symmetry:** All five records possess an existing Devotional Bhavartha view but are missing **both** Literal Translation and Traditional Meaning (Tatparya) views.
4. **Scholarly Scrutiny Accessibility:** Selected in English (`target_language = 'en'`) to permit cross-disciplinary review by both Vedic pandits and international Sanskrit philologists before regional propagation.

## 3. Individual Record Profiles & Commentarial Provenance

### Record 1: Mahamrityunjaya Mantra (Verse 1, Translation ID 5)
- **Source Scripture:** Rigveda Samhita 7.59.12; Shukla Yajurveda (Vajasaneyi Samhita) 3.60; Taittiriya Samhita 1.8.6.i.
- **Meter:** Anushtubh (8-8-8-8).
- **Rishi / Devata:** Maitravaruni Vasishtha / Rudra-Tryambaka.
- **Original Source Text:**
  ```text
  ॐ त्र्य॑म्बकं यजामहे सु॒गन्धिं॑ पुष्टि॒वर्ध॑नम् ।
  उ॒र्वा॒रु॒कमि॑व॒ बन्ध॑नान्मृ॒त्योर्मु॑क्षीय॒ माऽमृता॑त् ॥
  ```
- **Existing Bhavartha:** *"Om. We worship the Three-Eyed Lord (Shiva), who is fragrant and who nourishes all beings. Just as a ripe melon is released from its vine, may He liberate us from death and attachment, for the sake of Immortality."*
- **Missing Views to Generate:**
  1. `LITERAL`: Verbatim grammatical rendering preserving accusatives (*tryambakam*, *sugandhim*, *pushtivardhanam*), present plural middle (*yajāmahe*), ablative simile (*urvārukam iva bandhanāt*), and optative precative (*mṛkṣīya*).
  2. `TATPARYA`: Exegetical synthesis citing Sayana's RV Bhashya (interpreting *Tryambaka* as the Lord of the three worlds/three Ambikas) and Mahidhara's commentary on Vajasaneyi Samhita 3.60.

### Record 2: Gayatri Vedic Mantra (Verse 1, Translation ID 6)
- **Source Scripture:** Rigveda Samhita 3.62.10; Shukla Yajurveda 3.35; Taittiriya Aranyaka 10.27.
- **Meter:** Vedic Gayatri (8-8-8 with Tripada structure: 24 syllables).
- **Rishi / Devata:** Brahmarshi Vishvamitra / Savitar.
- **Original Source Text:**
  ```text
  ॐ भूर्भुव॒स्स॒वः॑ ।
  तत्स॑वि॒तुर्वरे॑ण्यं॒ भर्गो॑ दे॒वस्य॑ धीमहि ।
  धियो॒ यो नः॑ प्रचो॒दया॑त् ॥
  ```
- **Existing Bhavartha:** *"Om. We meditate upon the supreme, adorable radiance of the Divine Illuminator (Savitr). May that Divine Light inspire, guide, and illuminate our intellects and consciousness."*
- **Missing Views to Generate:**
  1. `LITERAL`: Word-by-word syntactical translation structured according to Anvaya (*devasya savituḥ tat vareṇyam bhargaḥ dhīmahi, yaḥ naḥ dhiyaḥ pracodayāt*).
  2. `TATPARYA`: Traditional commentarial synthesis citing Sayanacharya on RV 3.62.10 and Bhatta Bhaskara on Taittiriya Aranyaka, interpreting *Bhargas* as the ignorance-destroying inner light of consciousness.

### Record 3: Sri Suktam (Verse 1, Translation ID 567)
- **Source Scripture:** Rigveda Khilani 5.87 (Scheftelowitz 1906 critical edition).
- **Meter:** Anushtubh.
- **Rishi / Devata:** Ananda, Kardama, Chiklita / Sri-Lakshmi through Agni (Jatavedas).
- **Original Source Text:**
  ```text
  हि॒र॒ण्यव॑र्णां॒ हरि॑णीं सु॒वर्ण॑रजत॒स्रजा॑म् ।
  च॒न्द्रां हि॒रण्म॑यीं ल॒क्ष्मीं जात॑वेदो म॒ आव॑ह ॥ १ ॥
  ```
- **Existing Bhavartha:** *"O Jatavedas (Agni)! Invoke for me that Lakshmi who shines with the golden hue, who is graceful like a deer, adorned with garlands of gold and silver, luminous like the cool moon, and of golden essence."*
- **Missing Views to Generate:**
  1. `LITERAL`: Direct syntactical projection aligning adjectives (*hiraṇyavarṇām*, *hariṇīm*, *suvarṇa-rajata-srajām*, *candrām*, *hiraṇmayīm*) with accusative deity (*lakṣmīm*) and imperative verb (*āvaha*).
  2. `TATPARYA`: Theological synthesis citing the Vidyaranya/Sayana-school Sri Sukta Bhashya, explaining Agni as the divine invoker (*Hotr*) transmitting the prayer to Sri Mahalakshmi.

### Record 4: Purusha Suktam (Verse 1, Translation ID 724)
- **Source Scripture:** Rigveda Samhita 10.90.1; Vajasaneyi Samhita 31.1; Taittiriya Aranyaka 3.12.1.
- **Meter:** Anushtubh.
- **Rishi / Devata:** Narayana Rishi / Paramapurusha.
- **Original Source Text:**
  ```text
  सहस्र॑शीर्षा पुरु॑षः सहस्रा॒क्षः स॒हस्र॑पात् ।
  स भूमिं॑ वि॒श्वतो॑ वृ॒त्वात्य॑तिष्ठद्दशाङ्गु॒लम् ॥ १ ॥
  ```
- **Existing Bhavartha:** *"The Supreme Purusha has a thousand heads, a thousand eyes, and a thousand feet. Pervading the entire cosmos on all sides, He extends beyond it by ten fingers' length."*
- **Missing Views to Generate:**
  1. `LITERAL`: Verbatim rendering of Bahuvrihi compounds (*sahasra-śīrṣā*, *sahasrākṣaḥ*, *sahasrapāt*), active past imperfect (*atyatiṣṭhat*), and spatial accusative measure (*daśāṅgulam*).
  2. `TATPARYA`: Sayana's Rigveda Bhashya on RV 10.90.1, articulating that "thousand" indicates infinitude (*anantatva*), and "ten fingers" symbolizes transcendence beyond the physical universe (*brahmanda*).

### Record 5: Narayana Suktam (Verse 1, Translation ID 855)
- **Source Scripture:** Taittiriya Aranyaka 10.13.1 (Mahanarayana Upanishad 13.1 / Anandashrama 1894).
- **Meter:** Anushtubh.
- **Rishi / Devata:** Paramatman Narayana.
- **Original Source Text:**
  ```text
  स॒हस्र॑शीर्षं देवं॒ वि॒श्वाक्षं॑ वि॒श्वश॑म्भुवम् ।
  विश्वं॑ ना॒राय॑णं दे॒व॒म॒क्षरं॑ पर॒मं पदम् ॥ १ ॥
  ```
- **Existing Bhavartha:** *"We meditate upon the Supreme Lord Narayana, who has a thousand heads, who is all-seeing (with eyes everywhere), who is the source of all auspiciousness and happiness to the universe, who is the universe itself, imperishable, and the supreme goal."*
- **Missing Views to Generate:**
  1. `LITERAL`: Syntactical rendering mapping series of accusative attributes (*sahasra-śīrṣam*, *devam*, *viśvākṣam*, *viśva-śambhuvam*, *viśvam*, *nārāyaṇam*, *akṣaram*, *paramam padam*).
  2. `TATPARYA`: Bhatta Bhaskara Mishra's *Jnana Yajna* commentary and Sayana's Taittiriya Aranyaka Bhashya, explaining Narayana as the immanent and transcendent Brahman.
